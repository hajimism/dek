# 22. Lay boxes, pictures, and the text in them over a PPTX's picture as PowerPoint's own with dekc pptx --shapes

Date: 2026-10-26

## Status

Amended by [24. Make every dekc pptx lay out what PowerPoint draws as its own, and drop --shapes](0024-make-every-dekc-pptx-lay-out-what-powerpoint-draws-as-its-own-and-drop-shapes.md)

## Context

ADR 8 made a PPTX a picture of each slide with its text laid over it, line by line. A recipient fixes a word where it stands; nothing else moves.

A user handed a dek deck to people who had to keep editing it in PowerPoint, and rebuilt it by hand with pptxgenjs: rows as labels and rules, cards as gray rectangles with their bullets, a comparison as a table, icons as pictures of their own. What the recipients could not do with dek's file was move or recolor a card, change a cell of a table drawn as boxes, add a bullet to a card and have it wrap, or swap an icon. Those are pixels in ADR 8's picture.

The rebuild knew the deck's theme: this class is a card, that one a row. dek cannot: each project has its own theme, and a table is as often a grid of `<div>` cells as a `<table>`. What dek can know is how the browser drew each element, and most of what a dek theme draws is simple: a box with one color, a solid border, round corners. PowerPoint has those as shapes.

ADR 8 turned down a second renderer that maps all of CSS onto shapes and decides element by element what falls back to a picture. That still holds. What changes is that a small, fixed part of CSS maps exactly, and that a recipient who has to edit cards accepts a file where a substitute font can wrap a line where the browser did not.

## Decision

**`dekc pptx --shapes` lays boxes, pictures, and the text in them over the picture as PowerPoint's own.** Without the flag, `dekc pptx` is ADR 8's, byte for byte: the page is not numbered or measured for shapes at all. The measuring of text both share changes only where ADR 8's output was wrong: text that the `rotate`, `scale`, or `zoom` property turns or sizes, which ADR 8 meant to leave in the picture as it leaves text a `transform` rotates, and an alternative text's line break, which XML read back as a space. The flag is opt-in because text in a shape wraps where PowerPoint wraps it, which ADR 8's guarantee, the browser's line breaks, does not survive. `--json` keeps its shape, `outs`. The text output says, for each file, how many shapes and pictures it laid over the picture and names each slide where it laid none, the slides an author or an agent rewrites toward plain boxes.

**What becomes a picture of its own.** Every `<img>`, `<canvas>`, and outermost `<svg>` in the slide that is laid out, visible, and drawn upright, and that neither an ancestor's clipping nor the viewport cuts: the browser draws it alone, at twice the slide's size, on a transparent ground, as the slide's picture is drawn, with its own shadow and outline, and its frame reaches as far as they do. Decoration under `aria-hidden` is included: an icon is decoration and still something to swap. Its description is its `alt`, `aria-label`, or `<title>`.

An inline `<svg>` comes with the SVG as well, so PowerPoint shows the SVG and can turn it into shapes, and a renderer that cannot shows the PNG. Each element's computed paint is written onto it as attributes, so a theme's `currentColor` and tokens survive, each color as `rgb()` and an opacity, and the `<svg>`'s background color as a rectangle under the drawing. Nothing that runs goes into the file: scripts, event handler attributes, `<style>`, and dek's own markers are left out. The SVG is left out, and the PNG alone written, when the file would draw other than the page did: when it refers to anything outside itself; when the `<svg>` has a box of its own beyond a background color, such as padding, a border, a shadow, an outline, or a background with round corners, or a transform; when it or what it is in filters, blends, clips, or masks it; or when CSS set more on its elements than their attributes say, which is found by setting the file's elements where no stylesheet of the page reaches and comparing their computed style with the page's.

**What becomes a shape.** An element of the slide, not the slide itself, not a pseudo-element, and not a custom element, whose shadow root dek cannot see into, whose own paint is no more than:

- a background color, with no background image;
- borders that are solid: the same on every side, drawn as the shape's outline; or different per side, with square corners, drawn as a line per side;
- round corners, all four the same and circular, and no rounder than half a border is wide when they are round at all;
- no shadow, outline, filter, backdrop filter, clip path, mask, or blend mode;
- no transform, `scale`, `rotate`, or `zoom` but a uniform scale by `transform` or `scale`, and one box, not an inline broken across lines;
- nothing that an ancestor's clipping or the viewport cuts.

Its geometry is the border box, with the outline on the border's midline, as PowerPoint strokes a path; a corner radius is PowerPoint's `roundRect` adjustment, half the shorter side at most, which draws a pill or a circle. An elliptical corner, such as `50%` of a box that is not square, stays drawn.

**Nothing that stays in the picture may end up under a shape or a picture.** Shapes and pictures lie over the slide's picture, so whatever stays in that picture and the browser painted over them would be covered. A box or a picture is laid over only if nothing that stays drawn lies inside it, and nothing that stays drawn and might be painted after it overlaps it. What stays drawn: a box with any other paint, a custom element, what a pseudo-element draws, a list marker PowerPoint has no bullet for, text that ADR 8 leaves in the picture, a text's shadow, and a box or picture this rule keeps. A pseudo-element that Chromium does not place is taken to draw anywhere in its element. An element that a negative `z-index` puts beneath, and everything in it, stays drawn: what it lies under depends on whether its parent is a stacking context, which the order below does not record.

**Nor may two that are laid over be laid in an order the browser did not paint them in.** Where two that are laid over overlap and are written in an order the browser may not have painted them in, as grouping can force, the one written later stays drawn, or the one written earlier when the later is text, which is always laid over. Keeping one can keep another, so both rules run until nothing changes.

**Painted after is the browser's order as dek approximates it** (CSS 2, Appendix E). Where two elements' places in the document part, each paints in the stacking context they share at the layer of the first element from there that is positioned or a stacking context of its own: negative `z-index` first, then the flow, then positioned elements and `z-index: 0`, then positive `z-index`. In the flow, the backgrounds of blocks paint first, then floats, then inline content, inline blocks, flex and grid items, replaced content such as an image, and text; then the document's order decides. A line of text that holds more than one element's text paints as the latest of them, such as the text in a highlight over the highlight. Where an element further in is positioned or a stacking context too, it may paint in another context, and it is taken to be painted after only where its place in the document and its layers say so for every context it might be in.

**What a shape holds is grouped with it.** The shapes, pictures, and text inside an element that became a shape form one PowerPoint group with it, nested as the elements nest, so a card moves with its icon and its words. Everything is laid over in the order above.

**Text in a shape is the shape's, in paragraphs, when it reads as a column.** Text whose nearest shape is a box goes in that shape's text body when its blocks stand one under another, no line holds pieces set apart, no picture or other shape sits in its lines, and each block's lines stand at its line height apart, which a larger font in one line would break. Each block is a paragraph, with its line pitch, the space above it, its margins, and its runs, a `<br>`, and a newline that `white-space` keeps, as a line break. Its alignment is its `text-align`, unless its lines sit centered or flush right in its content box, as a flexbox or a grid sets them: what is measured wins. A list marker that is a disc, a circle, a square, or a decimal number from 1 to 32767 is the paragraph's bullet, hung where the browser drew the marker; numbered paragraphs that follow one another must count on by one, as one list's do, since PowerPoint numbers them as one list, and two lists, or a nested one, leave the text in lines. A margin is never below zero: what starts left of the content box, a bullet hung in the padding or a block a pixel out, moves the text's inset left, as far as the shape's own edge. The text is set from the top, its inset where its first line box starts, so that it grows down as it is edited, as PowerPoint's own text does; anchoring centered text in the middle was tried and dropped, since renderers center a line of exact spacing each in their own way, and the top lands where the browser set it in every one. A shape whose blocks the browser set each on one line does not wrap; one with a block the browser wrapped wraps at its width, its other blocks with it. Text in a shape that does not read as a column, and text in no shape, are line boxes as ADR 8 has them, in the shape's group if it has one.

**A group is as large as the frames of what it holds.** Its extent is the frames its parts are written with put together, a shape's path and a text box's room included, and its child space is the slide's, so nothing in it is scaled.

**The plan is apart from the page.** Functions in the page only measure: each element's paint, its place in the paint order and how it paints in the flow, each pseudo-element and marker, each block's text. A function with no page decides what is lifted, from those measurements alone, so every rule above is a unit test. Without `--shapes` the plan is the text boxes, in the order ADR 8 writes them. When the page takes what was lifted out of the slide's picture, or draws a picture alone, it turns transitions off, so the shot taken next is not of a change halfway done, and its rules outrank a theme's own `!important`.

**What CI checks, since PowerPoint is not there.** On the package: shapes, rules, pictures, and groups are written with the geometry and paint given, no margin below zero, every picture's PNG and SVG parts are typed and related, and every part parses. On the plan: each rule above, with measurements made up for it. In Chromium: a plain card becomes a shape with its text in paragraphs and its bullets; a card with a gradient highlight inside, and one a pseudo-element draws in, stay in the picture; a grid of cells becomes a shape a cell, centered marks centered; an inline SVG that paints with `currentColor` is written with the computed color; an icon is drawn alone on a transparent ground; a box with a transition is taken out of the slide's picture whole; a picture the slide cuts, a custom element, a rotation, and an ellipse stay drawn, and a picture with an outline is drawn with it; the text of a line is laid over a highlight in it; `pre-line` keeps its breaks; an SVG file carries no script, nor what its attributes cannot say; and every sample slide carries the same characters with `--shapes` as without it, each once. That the sample decks' files without `--shapes` are what they were before this decision, byte for byte, was checked by hand when this was written: the shape measuring does not run without the flag, and the text measuring both share changed only where ADR 8's output was wrong, which no sample slide reaches. That the sample decks' slides with `--shapes` validate against the ECMA-376 PresentationML schema was checked by hand too. Opening in a real renderer is checked by hand when this area changes, as ADR 8 has it. When this was written, macOS Quick Look drew the shapes, pictures, and text where the browser drew them, with two faults of its own in groups: a group's colors a step or two lighter, out of 255, and its lines a pixel off; shapes outside a group, and the spec's mapping of a group's child space, have neither.

## Consequences

A recipient can move, resize, and recolor a card or a cell, type into it and have the text wrap, and replace an icon, in a file that looks as the browser drew it until they do.

The rule decides per element, which ADR 8 avoided. It is one rule, written here and in the docs, and it errs toward the picture: a box is kept whenever something it would cover stays drawn, and whenever the order dek knows does not settle which of two paints over the other. A card with a gradient highlight in it, or a bullet drawn by `::before`, stays a picture with its text over it; writing bullets as list markers and highlights as plain colors makes it a shape. An element under a negative `z-index`, a custom element, and a badge positioned over a card it is not in can keep more in the picture than the browser needed.

Text in a shape is set by PowerPoint: a substitute font, or OpenType features such as `palt` that PowerPoint does not apply, can wrap a line where the browser did not, a one-line heading in a shape whose paragraph wraps among them. A cell whose text the browser centered top to bottom is set from the top, so a line added to it grows down rather than both ways. Without the flag nothing changes.

The approximation of paint order can be wrong where a flex or grid item's `order` changes the order it paints in, and where a stacking context nests in a way the keys do not record; the two rules keep most such cases in the picture.

The file is larger by a picture per image, icon, and diagram, at twice its size. dek still depends on nothing to write it.

There is no per-element report of what stayed drawn. The text output names the slides where nothing came out of the picture, which is where an agent rewriting toward shapes starts, and says how much each file lifted (Agent Usability Criteria 3 and 6). If agents need to know which element of a slide stayed and why, that is a new field in `--json` and a decision of its own.

`--shapes` is dek's from now on.
