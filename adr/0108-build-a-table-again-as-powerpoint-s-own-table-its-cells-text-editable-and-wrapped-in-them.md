# 108. Build a table again as PowerPoint's own table, its cells' text editable and wrapped in them

Date: 2026-11-19

## Status

Accepted

Amends [104. Build a PPTX again from JSON an agent writes, with dekc pptx spec and dekc pptx --from](0104-build-a-pptx-again-from-json-an-agent-writes-with-dekc-pptx-spec-and-dekc-pptx-from.md)

Amends [106. Say where text lies and what PowerPoint will wrap when a PPTX is built again, and keep a shared backdrop on the master](0106-say-where-text-lies-and-what-powerpoint-will-wrap-when-a-pptx-is-built-again-and-keep-a-shared-backdrop-on-the-master.md)

## Context

ADRs 104 and 106 leave tables and charts out of the JSON `dekc pptx --from` reads, each a decision of its own. A slide with a `<table>` is rebuilt as a box a cell or as a picture of the table: `why-dek`'s `agents` slide starts, in the `deck.pptx.json` `dekc pptx spec` writes, as 15 `prose` boxes for its 5 rows and 3 columns, and today's flow in ADR 106, with pptxgenjs, split tables into a box a word. A recipient who reworks the slide in PowerPoint wants a table there: to add a row, widen a column, or retype a cell and see the row grow.

Tables are common in the decks consultants hand over, and an HTML table's layout is a DrawingML table's: a grid of columns and rows, cells that start somewhere on it and reach across columns or down rows, each with its fill, its borders, its padding, and its text. Chromium has laid all of that out by the time `dekc pptx spec` reads the slide. What the outline lacked was the grid itself and where each cell lies on it: every cell's box was there, but not the columns' widths, a cell's spans, or an empty cell, which was in no node.

A chart is not the same kind of thing. PowerPoint's own chart holds its data and a workbook that opens in Excel, which neither the slide's HTML nor its outline has; it stays a picture of its part, and is a decision of its own.

## Decision

**The JSON has a `table` layer**: `x` and `y`, its top left corner; `columns`, each column's width from the left; and `rows`, each with its `height` and its `cells`. A row lists the cells that start in it, from the left, past those of the rows above that reach into it, as HTML places them; a cell says only what it sets: `columnSpan` and `rowSpan` (1), `fill`, `borders` per side (`width`, `paint`, `dash`), `inset` from its edges to its text (0), `anchor` (`top`, `middle`, or `bottom`; `top`), and `paragraphs`, as a shape's text has them (an empty cell). There is no `width` or `height`: the grid says them. The schema refuses cells that do not fill the grid once each, and says which row and column. A table may be held in a shape's `layers`, grouped with it.

**The writer writes PowerPoint's own table**: a `p:graphicFrame` holding an `a:tbl` with a `gridCol` a column and an `a:tr` a row, each row as high as the JSON says at least, since PowerPoint makes a row taller where its text needs more. Every place on the grid is an `a:tc`: where a merged cell starts it has `gridSpan` and `rowSpan`, and each place it covers says it is covered, `hMerge` from the left, `vMerge` from above, or both, with an empty paragraph. A border two cells share is one line in PowerPoint, so it is the wider of their two, the one above or to the left where they are as wide, as CSS collapses borders, and it is written on both cells. A merged cell's sides are written along each place it covers, and its far sides, right and bottom, on the place it starts at too, as PowerPoint writes a merge it made itself. A cell's insets are its margins, its anchor its anchor, and its fill and each side not set are written as none. The table has no table style, so it paints what its cells say and no banding; a recipient applies one from PowerPoint's gallery. An empty paragraph is as small as the table's smallest text, so an empty cell makes no row taller than the text in it does. `dekc pptx` writes no table, and its files are as they were.

**`spec.json` gives a table its grid.** A `<table>` node has `grid`: `x` and `y`, its first cell's corner, and `columns` and `rows`, each column's width and each row's height from one cell's edge to the next, what the layer's `columns` and `rows` are. Each `<td>` and `<th>` is a node, an empty one too, with `cell`: its `row` and `column` from 0, its `rowSpan` and `columnSpan` where it takes more than one, and its `anchor`, from its `vertical-align`. Cells are placed as HTML places them: a span stops at the end of its row group, `thead`, `tbody`, or `tfoot`, `rowspan="0"` too, and a row or cell the browser does not lay out, under `display: none`, takes no place on the grid. Its box, paint, `sides`, `padding`, and text are a node's as before. Both fields are new, and nothing that was there changes.

**`--from` finds a table's text in its cells.** For `pptx-text` and `pptx-place`, each cell with text is a box of its own, its columns and rows on the grid, the rows as high as the JSON says. Cells meet, so a text is in a cell only where it reaches 2 px into it, not within the 12 px a box is looked for in: a text set in the cell beside its own is away from where the slide shows it. For `pptx-fit`, a cell's text always wraps, inside its columns less its insets, the cell it lies in and not a narrower one beside it that holds the same characters: a text the browser set on one line in a cell narrower than its pieces' width times its `widen` warns, as a box that wraps does, and its hint names the column. `pptx-bounds` takes the grid's box. No check looks at a row's height: PowerPoint makes it what the text needs.

**What reads the file reads tables.** The PPTX eval counts a table as a shape and each of its cells with text as a body that wraps. The copy of the file `--shot` hands Quick Look multiplies a table's columns, rows, cell margins, and cell borders with every other length, and stops on a column or row it did not read.

**The skill `dek-pptx`** sets a `<table>` as one `table` layer from its node's `grid` and its cells' `cell`, `fill`, `border` or `sides`, `padding`, and text, in place of a `prose` a cell. As ADR 105 asks of a skill for newer work, it asks the installed dek first: where `deck.pptx.schema.json` has no `table` kind, it sets a `prose` a cell, as before.

**What CI checks.** In Chromium, a table with a cell across two columns, one down two rows, and an empty cell: its grid's columns and rows, each cell's place, spans, and anchor, the empty cell a node with its border, and the grid's corner the first cell's; and a span that stops at the end of its row group, `rowspan="0"` among them, and a hidden cell that takes no column. Without a browser: a table written out as JSON and read back is the same table; a cell left bare takes its defaults; the schema takes a cell that reaches into the row below and refuses one past the last column or row and a row short of columns; the writer's frame, grid, rows, merged and covered cells, margins, anchors, fills, a shared border written as the wider of two and as the left one when they are as wide, a merged cell's sides along what it covers and its far sides where it starts, and its covered cells' empty paragraphs; `pptx-text`, `pptx-place`, `pptx-fit`, and `pptx-bounds` on a table's cells, two cells beside each other swapped, and a cell measured against its own width beside a narrower one that says more; the eval's count of a table; and `magnified()` on a table's lengths.

## Consequences

`why-dek`'s `agents` slide was built again from `deck.pptx.json` with its 15 cell boxes replaced by one `table` layer written from `spec.json`'s grid and cells, by a script outside the repository. `--from` raised nothing on the slide but what the file started with, its picture of the rest and its code set a line a box. PowerPoint for Mac 16.115.1 opened the file without a repair; through AppleScript, slide 13 holds a 5 by 3 table whose rows are 23.64 and 24.02 pt high, as the browser laid them out, whose cells read as the slide does, and one of whose cells took new text. Quick Look's drawing of it differs from the browser's by 2.87 of 255. Its first drawing had each column's text stacked a character a line down the column's left edge, which is how the gap in `magnified()` was found. A hand-written table of three rows with a cell across two columns and one down two opened in PowerPoint the same way, its long cell's row taller where PowerPoint wrapped it. A merge PowerPoint made itself, of two by two cells in a file dek wrote, and saved, carries the merged cell's right and bottom sides on the cell it starts at and keeps each covered cell's sides as they were; Quick Look draws a merged cell's far sides from where it starts only, and drew none of them until the writer wrote them there too. That a merge's sides come out right in PowerPoint's own drawing was read from what it saved, not seen on screen.

A table is what a recipient edits as one, in PowerPoint's table tools: a cell's text wraps in it, and its row grows to hold it, as the hand-written table's did. The same growth moves the table's bottom edge when PowerPoint sets a cell's text wider than the browser did and wraps it, and nothing below the table moves with it; `pptx-fit` names such a cell before that happens, as it names a box.

What `a:tbl` cannot hold, the rebuild does without. Cells drawn apart with `border-spacing` meet: the gap is in the columns' widths and the rows' heights. A cell's round corners, shadow, gradient, or pseudo-element is not a cell's to draw, and stays the agent's choice of a part beside or behind the table. A `<caption>` is a `prose` of its own. A layout drawn with CSS grid or flexbox that looks like a table is not a `<table>` and gets no `grid`; an agent may still write it as a `table` layer from its boxes.

The `table` layer's fields join the JSON's contract (ADR 104), and `grid` and `cell` join `spec.json`'s (ADR 106): renaming one breaks every script that writes or reads it. Charts remain out of the JSON.
